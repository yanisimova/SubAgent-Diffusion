## Classification discipline

- **Strongest positive diffusion signal:** two original 2025 developer/technology-professional survey programs independently indicate high reported AI-tool exposure/use, but use distinct populations and definitions.
- **Most consequential inhibitor:** output trust and verification burden, compounded by security, confidentiality and integration demands in company contexts.
- **Weak claim rejected:** "AI coding assistants have crossed into the early majority across mid-size B2B SaaS firms." None of E1–E4 provide a representative firm-level adoption denominator, relevant peer stratification or longitudinal S-curve evidence. Its adopter-category classification is **indeterminate**.
- **Application-specific gap:** no source here measures *routine, successful, human-reviewed generation of unit tests and documentation* at comparable B2B SaaS firms. The agent may infer trialability from reversible workflows, not peer diffusion rates or positive ROI.
- **Time sensitivity:** 2025 evidence can go stale quickly by late 2026. Refresh with the newest original survey, sample method and peer case evidence before formal organizational decisions.

## Framework interpretation

Rogers' five perceived attributes are diagnostic hypotheses, not five independently measured causes in these sources. Relative advantage depends on local quality-adjusted time savings; compatibility on code/privacy rules; complexity on review overhead; trialability on an isolated pilot; observability on measured baseline comparison. Vendor promotion or intent to adopt must not be passed off as implemented use.
